Fly through the rings.

Flight & science

The shuttle and nearby particles already orbit Saturn. Thrusters change your relative motion; automatic counter-burns stop sideways or vertical drift when you release a control. There is no air drag, gravity-based jumping or fixed surface. At ten rocks, name your moon and press OK. A letterboxed launch follows; the shuttle controls are locked during this sequence. Above +48 m, you have five seconds to descend before the shuttle returns to a safe altitude. Color fades during the warning and returns when you descend. Your cargo stays with you. Escape / P pauses.

The flight model retains local orbital gravity, Coriolis terms and bounded thrusters, with exaggerated thrust strength for responsive play. The original curved ring panorama, small craft and rear chase camera use cinematic scale and travel speed; this view is not a metre-for-metre spacecraft simulation.

The main rings are typically about 10 m thick and mostly water ice, with dust and rock. This view enriches the original ring bands with a shallow volume of individual ice pieces. Widely spaced collectible rocks, a navigable corridor, fragments above and below the sheet, assisted speed and rapid coalescence are game design. It is not a spacecraft mission simulation. Pan orbits in the Encke gap; Daphnis in the narrower Keeler gap, both within A. Their displayed bodies are enlarged so you can see them. Their dated circular orbits use rounded JPL mean elements and are approximate, especially far from the reference epoch. B meets C directly: there is no broad opening between them. Cassini Division lies between B and A. Colors are muted ivory, gray and tan; grain size, ring thickness and the fictional moon launch remain stylized.
