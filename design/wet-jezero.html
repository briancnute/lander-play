Wet Jezero — compact shoreline activity proposal

October 7, 2026. Research and design recommendation for Q2. This is a proposal for producer review; no ancient Mars course or water physics is delivered.

Recommendation

Build one compact Jezero shoreline sprint: a rover route along an interpreted exposed delta margin, with water making the terrain and route choices visibly different. Reuse Jezero's steering, braking, boosts, pause/camera and immediate retry. Keep the modern compact relay and longer expedition available with their existing records.

The opening image should show a recognizable delta remnant and a broad lake together. The run then moves through three distinct decisions: choose a line around shore rocks, commit to an exposed terrace, and finish at an overlook where the lake opens into view. Aim first for a small route that earns a second run. Its eventual duration should follow terrain inspection and human playtesting, not an arbitrary required number of kilometres.

Recommend a rover-only first experiment. Adding the existing airlift could be considered if a crossing contributes a worthwhile decision; it should not be included merely to repeat the modern relay. A boat, submarine, swimming rover, vegetation or new collectible system would expand the work beyond this proposal.

What the evidence supports

Perseverance images show delta deposits and a history that includes high-energy floods capable of moving large boulders. The observations support an ancient river–lake system, with changes through time rather than one permanent water level. Mangold et al., Science (2021) is the primary research; NASA's accompanying explanation describes the stratigraphy and flood evidence.

Later rover work distinguishes multiple geological stages, including igneous crater-floor rocks, subsequent sediment deposition and changing water conditions. That history does not supply an exact ancient coastline for every modern landform. NASA's 2023 mission report summarizes those findings.

The current game's measured modern terrain is a useful starting shape, not a recovered lake-era landscape. Erosion, deposition and changing lake levels prevent “add one water plane” from becoming an exact reconstruction. Preserve recognizable terrain proportions and show a concise label: “An interpreted ancient Jezero shoreline, based on evidence of its former lake.” Avoid claims of a known exact year, inhabited Mars or an Earthlike breathable atmosphere. Ancient atmospheric density and precise water conditions remain uncertain; handling choices must be identified as game design.

First inspect the existing Kodiak/delta-margin area and discard any route that needs a long connecting drive. The first producer screenshot should compare the modern terrain and proposed wet interpretation from the same camera.

Construction gates and limits

Before building, identify a compact bounding area, measure candidate elevations in the actual game coordinate system, and sketch a proposed water level against it. Verify the main route and finish remain on exposed terrain, slopes stay traversable, and wet edges are readable from the driving camera. Keep major landforms' proportions: shorten quiet travel without squeezing their width while keeping their height. Do not flood the entire modern map or reshape the existing expedition.

For the initial experiment, water should be a clearly marked route boundary. Reuse automatic unsticking/return handling where compatible; entering the boundary must never yield an official shortcut time. Specify the time consequence and continuous input behavior during implementation, and test edge cases before release. Add no recovery button. A shallow-water driving mechanic would require a separate physics decision and evidence that it improves the activity.

Use one elapsed clock and immediate retry. Give the variant a separate course/save identity, with another version whenever route geometry or meaningful timing rules change. Wet-era records must not compete with modern expedition, relay or loop records. Keep entry direct from the Jezero Special's variant selection and independent of the calendar; no campaign gate, commute, mandatory discoveries or interrupting fact cards.

Validation and producer review

Construction would require route feasibility, water-edge and shortcut checks, automatic unsticking, pause timing, finish/retry, save/record isolation, desktop/phone controls and rendering cost checks. A reference rendering and matching in-game captures should establish the visual direction before expanding detail. Automated driving proves that a route can be completed, not that it is fun.

Producer run one: learn and finish; identify unclear water edges, sharp turns and empty stretches. Run two: try a faster line and report whether the scenery and decisions make this worth replaying alongside modern Jezero.
