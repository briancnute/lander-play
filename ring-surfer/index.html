About the rings

·

The shuttle and nearby particles already orbit Saturn. Thrusters change your relative motion; automatic counter-burns stop sideways or vertical drift when you release a control. There is no air drag, gravity-based jumping or fixed surface. At ten rocks, name your moon and press OK. Your moon shoots ahead and clears a channel through the rings. The camera follows from the craft until Saturn hides it, then flight resumes. Each later moon needs 20 rocks; the shuttle controls are locked during this sequence. Named moonlets remain in orbit and are labeled when visible. Above +48 m, you have five seconds to descend before the shuttle returns to a safe altitude. Color fades during the warning and returns when you descend. Your cargo stays with you. Escape / P pauses.

The flight model retains local orbital gravity, Coriolis terms and bounded thrusters, with exaggerated thrust strength for responsive play. The original curved ring panorama, small craft and rear chase camera use cinematic scale and travel speed; this view is not a metre-for-metre spacecraft simulation.

The main rings are typically about 10 m thick and mostly water ice, with dust and rock. Nearby rings resolve into irregular icy grains, sparse larger fragments and illustrative slanted clumps. Distant particles merge into bands; the near layer is porous, rather than a solid floor. Widely spaced collectible rocks, a navigable corridor, fragments above and below the sheet, assisted speed and rapid coalescence are game design. It is not a spacecraft mission simulation. The tiny map looks down from Saturn’s north pole: the gold arrow is your shuttle, gray dots are Pan and Daphnis, and the dark sector is Saturn’s dated shadow. The paused speed readout converts the on-screen motion to kilometres per second and explicitly marks it cinematic; it is not the local flight model’s 80 m/s cruise. Pan orbits in the Encke gap; Daphnis in the narrower Keeler gap, both within A. Their displayed bodies are enlarged so you can see them. Their dated circular orbits use rounded JPL mean elements and are approximate, especially far from the reference epoch. B meets C directly: there is no broad opening between them. Cassini Division lies between B and A. Colors are muted ivory, gray and tan; grain size, ring thickness and the fictional moon launch remain stylized.
