 THE SWING

Power. Then contact.

Gesture: pull down to set distance, then move forward along the same line and release near the starting point. A crooked backswing or return changes contact. Forward speed adds no power.

Three-click: click to start. Click again to lock distance. The marker continues to the far end, then returns—click in the small impact zone. Higher power means a faster return. A late power click sends the marker outward again before the accuracy pass. Miss a click entirely and the shot resets.

The flag on the bar marks target distance as a fraction of the selected club’s nominal range. It does not solve the slope, elevation or rollout. Harder shots shrink the impact window. Iron and wedge reward different landing choices.

During the shot: hold the speed button or Space to follow time faster. Release to return to normal.

 AUTHORED LUNAR PRACTICE

A little creative license.

The flag, Eagle, experiment area and Little West use approximate positions read from the 1978 Apollo 11 site map. Tee lies, crater depths and surrounding terrain are authored, not a surveyed reconstruction. The standing flag, cup, buggy and prepared surface are game fiction. Earth stays high in the western sky at Tranquility; dusk brings long shadows, not an Earthrise or an atmospheric sunset. Club launch conditions and dust friction are authored approximations. Flight uses lunar gravity and no aerodynamic forces.

The power bar represents nominal distance, not raw club speed. Chipping range means carry on level ground; putting range assumes the prepared flat surface. Bounce and slope change the final result.

Records stay on this device. Landing and Mars progress are separate.
