Room to leave the trail

The full recorded traverse fits inside this broad rectangle. At its extremes, the route has approximately 1.5–1.7 km of surrounding ground before the proposed edge. Players may explore between and beyond its bends wherever slopes permit.

This is an outer planning envelope. Cliffs, steep slopes and sandy terrain will reduce the usable area; it does not promise that every square metre is driveable.

What this establishes

  • All downloaded route segments are included, through sol 5021.
  • Landing-basin ground connects to the lower mountain ascent.
  • Mountain terrain continues beyond the southern and eastern edges.
  • Existing Gale arrival composition remains a continuity reference; its local coordinates still need registration to this terrain.
02 / THE SIZE DECISION

Target about two-thirds of Jezero’s area

A size budget, not a global shrink operation

The 9 × 17 km real envelope would become a 6 × 11.33 km equivalent budget at two-thirds spacing: 68 km², about one-third smaller than Jezero’s current 102.4 km² envelope. These dimensions express a target; no geographic-to-game transform has been designed.

Keep major landforms proportionate and shorten quiet connections between them. The final boundary may change shape. Uniform horizontal shrinking with unchanged heights would steepen terrain; that is not the construction proposal.

The uncompressed Gale crop is only 4.4% smaller than Jezero’s real footprint. Reaching the smaller game target requires stronger, selective distance editing—not merely drawing a smaller rectangle. Gale is also longer north–south, despite having less total area.

Keep the mountain’s scale

Build detailed driving ground around the recorded journey, with a broader measured mountain backdrop. A rewarding lower-slope climb can overlook the basin while higher terrain remains scenic. The playable edge should ultimately follow believable terrain limits.

Before construction

Survey finer terrain along Curiosity’s journey and candidate off-route connections; protect recognizable landmarks; test a floor-to-overlook section and the arrival view. Only then settle the compression, driveable boundary and climb ceiling.

Route integrity

The original NASA files are preserved alongside this survey. Their independent line parts are drawn without artificial connecting chords. No proposed future traverse is added. Retrieval date identifies the snapshot, not a live rover position.

Terrain evidence

The background is sampled from the USGS MSL Gale merged elevation mosaic v3, built from HRSC, CTX and HiRISE terrain tied to MOLA. Its 1 m output grid does not mean uniform 1 m source detail or accuracy. This survey uses one native sample per approximately 100 m cell; it is adequate for regional context, not small obstacles or driveability.

Coordinates use planetocentric latitude, east-positive longitude, a 3,396,190 m Mars sphere, and a local east/north frame centred at NASA’s first waypoint (137.441633° E, 4.589467° S). East distances include the cosine of that latitude. The same transform registers route, boundary and terrain.
