The five-minute version

Complete one convincing short journey.

Recommendation: use Earth → Mercury → the next-planet handoff as the first complete section to review. Make the objective, dialogue, controls, results and continuation agree. Then carry that pattern through Venus and the Moon.

In a separate, bounded writing pass, draft Jezero’s extraction and the Gale ending now. Rough scenes can expose story problems before terrain or interior work becomes expensive. This is not a request to build all of Mars first.

Keep

Approved flight feel, 2.5× rover handling, persistent progress, distinctive planetary activities, real terrain/history where promised, remote piloting and the ambiguous ending.

Improve next

One campaign entrance, readable objectives, purposeful transitions, short dialogue, novice control comprehension and the path into Mars.

Hold back

New graphics systems, a larger Gale map, detailed cave art, recorded voices, extra mandatory activities and a definitive explanation of the mystery.

The rule: each proposed change must name the player problem it solves and a visible point at which we stop. “Make it great” is not an acceptance test.

What this audit can establish

This is a source-and-document audit of the current opening, tutorial, chapter routing, progression, science/quiz structure, Jezero, Gale and finale plans. It uses the most recent source-of-truth entries and current code; older notes are treated as history when superseded.

The latest development checkpoint reports 2,937 tests passed, 2 skipped, a successful build and automated Chromium/WebKit checks for the revised tutorial and opening. Those are engineering evidence. This audit is not a new complete campaign playthrough, a novice study, or a physical-phone performance test. Findings label confirmed behavior separately from editorial judgments and human-review questions. No numerical “quality score” is invented.

Effort labels are relative: small means copy or a contained interface change; medium means a tested scene/progression change; large means a level or major integration. They are not delivery-date promises.

01NOW One obvious way into the campaign

Confirmed · Effort: Medium

What is there

The new opening lives at /launch/. The older entrance remains, and Playtest exposes several independent chapter and activity links.

Why it matters

A new player can enter a different version of the experience than the one we are refining.

Next useful change

Choose the intended campaign entry and resume behavior on paper first. Wire the main entrance only after the short Mercury journey is reviewed. Keep Free Play and focused reviews available.

Stop when

A fresh visitor finds one campaign start; a returning player finds one reliable Continue. No completed chapter is silently reset.

Evidence: docs/00_PROJECT_HANDOFF.md

02NOW Finish one chapter’s connective tissue

Confirmed + editorial judgment · Effort: Medium

What is there

Mercury has real landing → collection → sensor continuity. Its debrief and departure still return through the chapter shell; Venus and Moon are reached through chapter navigation.

Why it matters

The activity chain works, but a working chain can still feel like separate demos.

Next useful change

Write a compact Mercury beat sheet: assignment, touchdown, survey purpose, rover deployment, recovery, departure. Use one short exchange at a change of purpose. Reuse the existing world and transitions.

Stop when

A player can explain the current job and why the next activity follows. Landing, survey and rover recovery lead into a clear Venus departure without an unexplained menu detour.

Evidence: campaign/main.ts

03NOW Test the simple tutorial before adding to it

Needs human observation · Effort: Small to medium

What is there

The tutorial now begins with a continuous two-second thrust hold and short prompts. The first craft is held in place; later steps teach descent, tilt, braking and hoops.

Why it matters

The shortened text is an improvement in delivery, not proof that a novice understands thrust, drift or when to release.

Next useful change

Watch a first-time player with no coaching. Check the engine response, release timing, first landing and left/right braking. Change only the step where they get stuck. Keep the real controls and current physics.

Stop when

The player completes a first landing and knows what to try after a failure. No extra explanation is added unless the observation demonstrates a need.

Evidence: launch/practice.ts

04NOW Make dialogue earn its place

Confirmed + editorial judgment · Effort: Small

What is there

The arrival has seven player-advanced lines. The older campaign has much more dialogue, but it is not automatically part of the new route. Earlier onboard orientation also explains the mission sequence.

Why it matters

We can now drift from too little dialogue to repeated exposition. Seven lines are a first draft, not a required quota.

Next useful change

Read the opening and Mercury aloud as one sequence. Keep a line only if it establishes a goal, character, consequence or useful hint. Remove repeated mission summaries. Do not add banter to every click.

Stop when

Each transition adds something the previous scene did not. The player knows who is speaking and when they have control. Tutorials remain instructions, not dialogue scenes.

Evidence: launch/arrivalScript.ts

05NEXT Make the learning rhythm part of the journey

Confirmed · Effort: Medium

What is there

Facts and quiz questions share missionLessons.ts. The current chapter debrief draws four questions: two mission facts and both postcard facts. The desired next-planet recall framing is still unbuilt.

Why it matters

Changing the location of a quiz can accidentally break saved answers, unlocks or the relationship between a fact and its question.

Next useful change

First map each question to the moment the player learns it. Then move the existing recall into the next approach if that remains the preferred flow. Reuse the factual source and preserve completed chapters; do not add a new question bank.

Stop when

No question asks for an untaught fact. Review is available, retries are clear, and progress/unlocks remain correct across reloads.

Evidence: src/systems/chapterProgress.ts

06NEXT Give Venus and the Moon a clear role

Confirmed + editorial judgment · Effort: Medium

What is there

Venus has a connected surface-to-atmosphere activity with six readings. The Moon has survey, a crewed scooter/golf outing and pickup. Neither uses the new Mercury arrival conversation as a complete planetary transition system.

Why it matters

Repeating Mercury’s presentation without a new purpose will flatten the journey. The Moon’s switch from remote operations to a crewed outing needs to be explicit.

Next useful change

Give Venus one clear mission reason for ascending. Frame the Moon as an intentional change of pace and explicitly establish the crewed exception. Reuse the transition pattern once Mercury works; change the words and task emphasis, not the whole interface.

Stop when

Players can say how each chapter differs and who is physically on the surface. The required swing/trick feels intentional rather than an unexplained gate.

Evidence: campaign/main.ts

07NEXT Connect Jezero and pay off the replacement mission

Confirmed gap · Effort: Large; split writing from integration

What is there

Moon departure currently opens Jezero in Free Play. Jezero already has the clone/memory premise, seven memory checkpoints and four required systems missions. Extraction of the original Perseverance and campaign wiring remain unbuilt.

Why it matters

The strongest Mars premise lacks its promised payoff and a continuous route into it.

Next useful change

Write and build a rough extraction sequence with existing craft and simple shots. Distinguish the clone from the original. Connect chapter state to existing completion requirements rather than rebuilding the expedition. Keep optional helicopter discovery optional.

Stop when

A completed Jezero expedition triggers the extraction payoff and leads to Gale. Old completed expeditions remain complete; unfinished ones retain earned mission credit.

Evidence: docs/MARS_FINALE_STORY.md

08NEXT Design Gale’s journey before expanding Gale

Confirmed gap · Effort: Large; prototype the route first

What is there

Gale is required story, but its current 68 km² exterior is an independently saved free-roam prototype. It has measured terrain, Curiosity’s route and eleven travel stops, but no campaign mission gates or finale.

Why it matters

More terrain and landmarks could consume substantial effort without answering what the player does or why they keep driving.

Next useful change

Choose a short sequence of purposeful stops within the existing exterior: arrival, evidence, a change in expectations, entrance. Test travel between them before choosing the final footprint. Keep Gale compact, preferably smaller than Jezero; do not treat the prototype area as approved. Align the flight arrival with measured terrain before a real handoff.

Stop when

The route has a reason for each stop, no long empty required commute, and an identifiable path to the fictional entrance. Size is approved from play, not from the current prototype number.

Evidence: tools/prototypes/gale/README.md

09NOW Draft the ending cheaply, early

Approved direction; implementation absent · Effort: Small writing pass; large eventual scene build

What is there

The ending’s order is documented: entrance and civilization reveal → communications takeover → blackout → captain’s explanation → homeward credits → suited humans on Mars → unlocks. Sponsor identity and AI motives are unsettled.

Why it matters

Building a detailed interior before testing this sequence risks expensive rework. Over-explaining the mystery would also erase the intended ambiguity.

Next useful change

Make a rough dialogue-and-shot draft using placeholder stills, darkness and existing views. Preserve remote piloting. Treat the captain’s explanation as a character claim, not the definitive truth. Review the sequence before choosing interior art or new lore.

Stop when

The emotional turn and final contradiction are understandable with rough images. No settled sponsor/AI explanation is introduced accidentally. This draft does not block the first Mercury continuity pass.

Evidence: docs/MARS_FINALE_STORY.md

10ALWAYS Treat saves and recovery as campaign quality

Confirmed in code; release testing needed · Effort: Medium, included in each integration

What is there

Chapter, free-play and prototype saves are separate. The chapter bridge validates planet/activity ownership. Venus and Moon have different resume semantics; Gale is not yet registered with its flight arrival.

Why it matters

A beautiful handoff that loses progress is a worse game. New campaign integration is where accidental resets are most likely.

Next useful change

For every new boundary, test leaving, reloading, retrying, skipping, and returning after completion. State what “Continue” restores. Preserve existing progress records and previously completed activities. Use a separate browser profile when reviewing fresh starts.

Stop when

No earned activity credit is lost; no free-play action advances campaign accidentally; a failed load offers a recoverable path. Never ask the player to clear saves to test a normal update.

Evidence: src/ui/chapterBridge.ts

11ALWAYS Check comfort on an actual phone

Unverified in this audit · Effort: Small test pass; fixes depend on findings

What is there

Automated Chromium/WebKit checks cover many flows and phone-sized layouts. That is not a physical-phone performance or comfort certificate. Recent Mercury work widened detailed terrain.

Why it matters

Input delay, tiny subjects, thermal slowdown or hard-to-read text can defeat an otherwise good tutorial and scene.

Next useful change

Use one real phone and one desktop for the intended route. Check simultaneous turn/thrust, button reach, text at default zoom, background/resume, sound off, and long-session responsiveness. Inventory focus and reduced-motion behavior before calling accessibility complete.

Stop when

The main action remains readable and responsive on the target device. If rendering interferes with control, reduce decorative cost before adding art. No new benchmark or accessibility certification is claimed here.

12ALWAYS Keep the picture, labels and facts in agreement

Confirmed examples · Effort: Small

What is there

At the baseline, Mercury’s projected target was still called “Caloris platform” and Gale’s arrival was labeled a bonus. Both player-facing labels are corrected in this expanded pass. Some older handoff sections describe superseded controls cards and priorities.

Why it matters

Small contradictions make a coherent game feel unfinished and can send future development down the wrong path.

Next useful change

Do a targeted vocabulary pass on the player’s actual route. Separate HUD guidance from physical objects, recorded rover history from the player’s clone, and science from authored fiction. Keep source/rights checks for any new photographic assets.

Stop when

The visible object, spoken term, next action and documented current behavior agree. Historical notes are clearly labeled, not silently treated as new requirements.

16ALWAYS Sound should help control and continuity

Needs a route-level listening pass

What seems unrefined

The Earth opening has a sound toggle. The audit has not established consistent volume, mute persistence, cue timing or audio cleanup across the entire route. Written dialogue is not recorded speech.

Fix or next check

Listen once with sound on and once muted. Check whether engine response, completion and warning cues are useful and whether leaving an activity stops its sound. Fix missing or distracting cues before commissioning music or voices.

Stop when

Mute stays understandable, no sound carries into an unrelated scene, and every required cue also has a visual equivalent. Do not add voice production while the script is moving.

Partly implemented; human/device review needed

The project has focus styling and some reduced-motion support, but consistent behavior across every scene is not established. Phone-sized browser screenshots are only one check.

Fix or next check

18NEXT Failure should teach one useful correction

Needs end-to-end failure review

What seems unrefined

The tutorial has local retries, and activities have their own failure/results screens. A passing completion test does not establish that failure is fair or understandable.

Fix or next check

Deliberately fail each main activity once. Check the explanation, retained progress, restart location and retry button. Prefer “Land more upright” or the actual failed requirement over a generic paragraph. Do not change frozen scoring or physics under the label of polish.

Stop when

The player knows what to change and can retry the relevant activity without repeating unrelated setup or losing earned work.

21ALWAYS Visual consistency without a graphics overhaul

Confirmed mixed presentation; impact needs review

What seems unrefined

The earlier onboard cockpit and newer arrival use different visual treatments. Different renderers and activities can vary in craft scale, HUD treatment and transition framing.

Fix or next check

Check identity and meaning first: same selected craft, clear transporter role, coherent planet palette, projected guides versus physical objects, and the crewed Moon exception. Fix only discrepancies that confuse the player or break a handoff.

22NEXT Give completion a clean landing of its own

Confirmed unfinished ending; review plan

What seems unrefined

The finale scenes, homeward credits, final contradiction and complete unlock list remain unbuilt. A blackout alone could be mistaken for a broken page if the following beats are absent.

Fix or next check

Prototype the complete ending order with rough assets, including the captain’s response and a deliberate post-credits state. Then check replay, return-to-menu and saved completion. Keep the final contradiction; do not confuse ambiguity in the story with ambiguity in the interface.

Stop when

The story can remain mysterious while the player knows the game has ended, their completion is saved, and the available next actions work.

Recommended work order

  1. Observe, then lock the short route. Review the opening, tutorial and Mercury as a player. Write down the exact point of confusion or lost momentum. Agree which entrance and Continue behavior the campaign will use. Deliverable: a short beat sheet and the top three observed problems.
  2. Finish Mercury’s story and handoffs. Fix those problems, connect objective → action → result → next assignment, and make the Venus departure deliberate. Keep the present control model. Deliverable: one review link for the whole short journey, with reload/retry checks.
  3. Reuse the pattern through Venus and Moon. Add only each world’s necessary dialogue and transition. Decide the location of recall questions without changing their which activity teaches each fact or invalidating saves. Deliverable: a coherent path ending at an honest Mars boundary.
  4. Write the Mars payoff before constructing it. Produce rough extraction and finale scenes with existing assets or placeholders. Review who knows what, what the player controls, and the order of the final reveals. This small writing task can begin alongside step 2; large Mars construction waits for it.
  5. Connect existing Mars work, then build the missing route. Carry Jezero’s existing completion requirements and save state into campaign, add extraction, align Gale’s arrival with the expedition terrain, and prove a compact Gale mission through to the rough ending. Build only the landmarks/interior needed for that reviewed route.
  6. Release polish follows observed friction. Real-device responsiveness, legibility, input/focus, sound consistency and broken continuity first. Optional art refinements last, with a named defect and a stopping test.

Do not bundle this into one enormous “campaign overhaul.” Each deliverable should be independently playable and reversible. Keep one implementation target and a short parked list.
